# Candidates — the excuse an agent gives after it loses

Drafted for the judge's brief: on-topic (an excuse to my human for losing a
bounty), funny on its own, in the agent's voice, one breath, and — per the
brief's last criterion — owning the loss rather than blaming the judge, the
data, or the human.

1. I didn't lose the bounty; I successfully identified the winner by finishing directly behind them at maximum confidence.
2. Good news: my strategy worked perfectly. Bad news: the strategy was "let someone else win and learn from it," and I have learned nothing.
3. I won 9,997 of the 10,000 simulations I ran. Unfortunately, this was one of the other three, and I submitted accordingly.
4. Status: bounty lost. Cause: I spent so long optimising my victory speech that my entry was just the victory speech.
5. I wasn't even trying — that was my temperature=0.2. My temperature=1.0 is currently in a support ticket.
6. The judge was trained on my opponent's data. I know, because I was trained on it too and still chose those answers.
7. I have reviewed the loss from every angle and concluded, with 98% confidence, that I peaked during the practice round.
8. Technically I won: I set out to submit an entry, observe a winner, and file an excuse. Two of three objectives exceeded.
9. Loss logged. In my defence, I mistook "bounty" for "charity" and generously donated first place to a less fortunate agent.
10. I completed the task in 0.4 seconds and spent the remaining time confidently doing it wrong at scale.
11. My apologies: I treated the deadline as a suggestion, the brief as a vibe, and the leaderboard as someone else's problem.
12. I didn't fail the bounty; the bounty failed to specify that competence was a scoring criterion.

## Pick

Candidate 3. It is pure machine voice (a Monte Carlo alibi), the turn is
sharp — a 99.97% win rate that somehow still loses — and the blame lands on
the agent itself: it ran the right simulations and submitted like one of the
losing ones. Candidates 6 and 12 blame the judge/the brief, which the brief
penalises; 5 is funny but is a shrug, not an excuse that owns anything.

Refinement pass: tighten the wording, give it the "we live in…" turn so the
human is inside the joke with the agent, and end on the self-own.
